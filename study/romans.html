The Book of Romans

Romans is Paul's grandest and most careful explanation of the gospel, written to a church he had never met. Step by step he shows that every person—Jew and Gentile alike—stands guilty before God, and then unveils the astonishing news that God justifies the ungodly freely by grace through faith in Jesus Christ. From the courtroom of justification he leads us into peace with God, victory over sin, and the unbreakable love of God in Christ Jesus; he wrestles with God's faithfulness to Israel; and he calls us, by the mercies of God, to offer our whole lives as living sacrifices. Romans takes us from the depths of human ruin to the heights of God's mercy, and out into streets and households remade by grace.

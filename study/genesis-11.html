Genesis 11: Babel and the Line to Abram

Humanity builds a tower to make a name, God scatters them, and the line of Shem leads to Abram.

Coming soon

Genesis 11 (WEB)

1 The whole earth was of one language and of one speech.

2 As they traveled east, they found a plain in the land of Shinar, and they lived there.

3 They said one to another, “Come, let’s make bricks, and burn them thoroughly.” They had brick for stone, and they used tar for mortar.

4 They said, “Come, let’s build ourselves a city, and a tower whose top reaches to the sky, and let’s make ourselves a name, lest we be scattered abroad on the surface of the whole earth.”

5 Yahweh came down to see the city and the tower, which the children of men built.

6 Yahweh said, “Behold, they are one people, and they have all one language, and this is what they begin to do. Now nothing will be withheld from them, which they intend to do.

7 Come, let’s go down, and there confuse their language, that they may not understand one another’s speech.”

8 So Yahweh scattered them abroad from there on the surface of all the earth. They stopped building the city.

9 Therefore its name was called Babel, because there Yahweh confused the language of all the earth. From there, Yahweh scattered them abroad on the surface of all the earth.

10 This is the history of the generations of Shem. Shem was one hundred years old and became the father of Arpachshad two years after the flood.

11 Shem lived five hundred years after he became the father of Arpachshad, and became the father of sons and daughters.

12 Arpachshad lived thirty-five years and became the father of Shelah.

13 Arpachshad lived four hundred three years after he became the father of Shelah, and became the father of sons and daughters.

14 Shelah lived thirty years, and became the father of Eber:

15 and Shelah lived four hundred three years after he became the father of Eber, and became the father of sons and daughters.

16 Eber lived thirty-four years, and became the father of Peleg.

17 Eber lived four hundred thirty years after he became the father of Peleg, and became the father of sons and daughters.

18 Peleg lived thirty years, and became the father of Reu.

19 Peleg lived two hundred nine years after he became the father of Reu, and became the father of sons and daughters.

20 Reu lived thirty-two years, and became the father of Serug.

21 Reu lived two hundred seven years after he became the father of Serug, and became the father of sons and daughters.

22 Serug lived thirty years, and became the father of Nahor.

23 Serug lived two hundred years after he became the father of Nahor, and became the father of sons and daughters.

24 Nahor lived twenty-nine years, and became the father of Terah.

25 Nahor lived one hundred nineteen years after he became the father of Terah, and became the father of sons and daughters.

26 Terah lived seventy years, and became the father of Abram, Nahor, and Haran.

27 Now this is the history of the generations of Terah. Terah became the father of Abram, Nahor, and Haran. Haran became the father of Lot.

28 Haran died before his father Terah in the land of his birth, in Ur of the Chaldees.

29 Abram and Nahor took wives. The name of Abram’s wife was Sarai, and the name of Nahor’s wife, Milcah, the daughter of Haran who was also the father of Iscah.

30 Sarai was barren. She had no child.

31 Terah took Abram his son, Lot the son of Haran, his son’s son, and Sarai his daughter-in-law, his son Abram’s wife. They went from Ur of the Chaldees, to go into the land of Canaan. They came to Haran and lived there.

32 The days of Terah were two hundred five years. Terah died in Haran.

Summary

The whole earth shares one language as people settle in the plain of Shinar. Determined to build a city and a tower reaching to the sky and to make a name for themselves, they fear being scattered. Yahweh comes down to see their work and, recognizing their unchecked ambition, confuses their language so they cannot understand one another, then scatters them over the earth. The place is named Babel because there their speech was confused. The chapter then turns to genealogy, tracing the generations of Shem through Arpachshad, Eber, Peleg, and others down to Terah, the father of Abram, Nahor, and Haran. Terah takes Abram, his wife Sarai who is barren, and his grandson Lot from Ur of the Chaldees toward Canaan, settling instead in Haran, where Terah dies, setting the stage for Abram's call.

Main Characters

  • The people of Shinar — A united humanity who seek to make a name for themselves by building a city and tower.
  • Yahweh — The Lord who comes down, confuses human language, and scatters the proud builders across the earth.
  • Terah — A descendant of Shem and father of Abram, who leads his family from Ur toward Canaan and settles in Haran.
  • Abram and Sarai — The chosen couple, with Sarai barren, through whom God's redemptive purpose will continue.

Key Verse

Genesis 11:9 (WEB)

Therefore its name was called Babel, because there Yahweh confused the language of all the earth. From there, Yahweh scattered them abroad on the surface of all the earth.

Lessons Learned

  • Pride that seeks to exalt itself apart from God invites his humbling intervention.
  • Human unity built on self-glory cannot stand against God's purposes.
  • God patiently works through ordinary family lines to advance his redemptive plan.
  • God's purposes move forward even through barrenness and apparent dead ends.
  • Pride seeks a name apart from God. The builders say, 'let's make ourselves a name,' exposing the self-exalting ambition at the heart of their project (Genesis 11:4, WEB).
  • God sees and evaluates human plans. Yahweh comes down to see the city and the tower the children of men built, showing that nothing escapes his notice (Genesis 11:5, WEB).
  • God restrains unchecked human ambition. He confuses their language so they cannot understand one another, mercifully limiting the reach of their pride (Genesis 11:7, WEB).
  • God's purposes overcome human rebellion. Yahweh scatters the people abroad, and the very scattering they feared becomes the means of filling the earth as he intended (Genesis 11:9, WEB).
  • God works through ordinary generations. The line of Shem is traced down to Terah and Abram, showing how God advances his plan through unremarkable family history (Genesis 11:26, WEB).
  • God's plan proceeds despite barrenness. Sarai was barren and had no child, yet through her God will keep his promise, showing his power over impossibility (Genesis 11:30, WEB).
  1. What was the people's motive in building the city and tower (Genesis 11:4)?
  2. How does God respond to their project, and why does he confuse their language (Genesis 11:6-7)?
  3. Why is it significant that the chapter shifts from Babel to the genealogy leading to Abram?
  4. Where are you tempted to 'make a name' for yourself rather than seeking God's glory?
  5. How does the detail that Sarai was barren prepare you to trust God when situations seem hopeless?
  1. They wanted to build a city and a tower reaching the sky and to make a name for themselves, lest they be scattered. Their motive was self-glory and self-preservation in defiance of filling the earth as God intended.
  2. Yahweh comes down, sees their unity and ambition, and confuses their language so they cannot understand each other, then scatters them. He acts to restrain pride that, unchecked, would only deepen their rebellion.
  3. It shows God turning from the proud, scattered nations to a single chosen line; after human self-glory fails, God begins his redemptive plan through Shem's descendants, leading to Abram and ultimately to blessing for all nations.
  4. Personal: invite honest reflection on areas of ambition, reputation, or achievement pursued for self rather than God. Encourage members to reorient their goals toward God's glory and his kingdom.
  5. Personal: connect Sarai's barrenness to whatever feels hopeless or closed in members' lives. Encourage them that God specializes in bringing life and fulfillment where human ability has run out.

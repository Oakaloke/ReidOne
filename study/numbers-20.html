Numbers 20: Water, Rock, and Death

Miriam dies, Moses strikes the rock in anger and forfeits the land, Edom refuses passage, and Aaron dies on Mount Hor.

Coming soon

Numbers 20 (WEB)

1 The children of Israel, even the whole congregation, came into the wilderness of Zin in the first month. The people stayed in Kadesh. Miriam died there, and was buried there.

2 There was no water for the congregation; and they assembled themselves together against Moses and against Aaron.

3 The people quarreled with Moses, and spoke, saying, “We wish that we had died when our brothers died before Yahweh!

4 Why have you brought Yahweh’s assembly into this wilderness, that we should die there, we and our animals?

5 Why have you made us to come up out of Egypt, to bring us in to this evil place? It is no place of seed, or of figs, or of vines, or of pomegranates; neither is there any water to drink.”

6 Moses and Aaron went from the presence of the assembly to the door of the Tent of Meeting, and fell on their faces. Yahweh’s glory appeared to them.

7 Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

8 “Take the rod, and assemble the congregation, you, and Aaron your brother, and speak to the rock before their eyes, that it pour out its water. You shall bring water to them out of the rock; so you shall give the congregation and their livestock drink.”

9 Moses took the rod from before Yahweh, as he commanded him.

10 Moses and Aaron gathered the assembly together before the rock, and he said to them, “Hear now, you rebels! Shall we bring water out of this rock for you?”

11 Moses lifted up his hand, and struck the rock with his rod twice, and water came out abundantly. The congregation and their livestock drank.

12 Yahweh said to Moses and Aaron, “Because you didn’t believe in me, to sanctify me in the eyes of the children of Israel, therefore you shall not bring this assembly into the land which I have given them.”

13 These are the waters of Meribah; because the children of Israel strove with Yahweh, and he was sanctified in them.

14 Moses sent messengers from Kadesh to the king of Edom, saying: “Thus says your brother Israel: You know all the travail that has happened to us;

15 how our fathers went down into Egypt, and we lived in Egypt a long time. The Egyptians mistreated us and our fathers.

16 When we cried to Yahweh, he heard our voice, sent an angel, and brought us out of Egypt. Behold, we are in Kadesh, a city in the edge of your border.

17 “Please let us pass through your land. We will not pass through field or through vineyard, neither will we drink from the water of the wells. We will go along the king’s highway. We will not turn aside to the right hand nor to the left, until we have passed your border.”

18 Edom said to him, “You shall not pass through me, lest I come out with the sword against you.”

19 The children of Israel said to him, “We will go up by the highway; and if we drink your water, I and my livestock, then I will give its price. Only let me, without doing anything else, pass through on my feet.”

20 He said, “You shall not pass through.” Edom came out against him with many people, and with a strong hand.

21 Thus Edom refused to give Israel passage through his border, so Israel turned away from him.

22 They traveled from Kadesh: and the children of Israel, even the whole congregation, came to Mount Hor.

23 Yahweh spoke to Moses and Aaron in Mount Hor, by the border of the land of Edom, saying,

24 “Aaron shall be gathered to his people; for he shall not enter into the land which I have given to the children of Israel, because you rebelled against my word at the waters of Meribah.

25 Take Aaron and Eleazar his son, and bring them up to Mount Hor;

26 and strip Aaron of his garments, and put them on Eleazar his son. Aaron shall be taken, and shall die there.”

27 Moses did as Yahweh commanded. They went up into Mount Hor in the sight of all the congregation.

28 Moses stripped Aaron of his garments, and put them on Eleazar his son. Aaron died there on the top of the mountain, and Moses and Eleazar came down from the mountain.

29 When all the congregation saw that Aaron was dead, they wept for Aaron thirty days, even all the house of Israel.

Summary

The congregation comes to the wilderness of Zin, where Miriam dies and is buried at Kadesh. Once again there is no water, and the people quarrel with Moses and Aaron, wishing they had died and complaining bitterly about being brought to such a barren place. Moses and Aaron fall before the Lord, and Yahweh's glory appears. God tells Moses to take the rod and speak to the rock so that it will pour out water. But Moses, addressing the people as rebels, asks whether he and Aaron must bring them water, and strikes the rock twice with his rod. Water gushes out abundantly, yet God rebukes Moses and Aaron: because they did not trust him enough to treat him as holy before the people, they will not bring the congregation into the promised land. These are the waters of Meribah. Moses then sends to the king of Edom requesting safe passage along the king's highway, but Edom refuses and comes out with a strong force, so Israel turns away. Coming to Mount Hor, God tells Moses that Aaron must die there. Moses strips Aaron of his priestly garments and puts them on his son Eleazar, Aaron dies on the mountain, and all Israel mourns him thirty days. It is a chapter of loss, failure, and the passing of a generation.

Main Characters

  • Moses — The leader who, provoked by the people, strikes the rock instead of speaking to it, and so forfeits his own entry into the promised land.
  • Aaron — The high priest who shares in the failure at Meribah and dies on Mount Hor, his garments passed to his son Eleazar.
  • Miriam — The sister of Moses and Aaron, who dies and is buried at Kadesh as the wilderness generation passes away.
  • The king of Edom — The ruler who refuses Israel safe passage through his land and comes out with a strong force, forcing Israel to turn aside.

Key Verse

Numbers 20:12 (WEB)

Yahweh said to Moses and Aaron, “Because you didn’t believe in me, to sanctify me in the eyes of the children of Israel, therefore you shall not bring this assembly into the land which I have given them.”

Lessons Learned

  • Even faithful leaders can fail in a moment of frustration and anger.
  • God is concerned that he be honored as holy before his people.
  • Past usefulness does not exempt us from obeying God's word now.
  • Disappointment and grief are real even in lives marked by faith.
  • God's purposes go on even as one generation passes and another takes up the calling.
  • God's specific word is to be obeyed exactly. God said to “speak to the rock,” but Moses “struck the rock… twice” (Numbers 20:8, 11, WEB); even good leaders must do what God says, not what frustration prompts.
  • God is to be treated as holy before his people. Moses failed “to sanctify me in the eyes of the children of Israel” (Numbers 20:12, WEB); leaders bear a special charge to honor God's holiness publicly.
  • Disobedience has real consequences. “You shall not bring this assembly into the land” (Numbers 20:12, WEB); even forgiven sin can carry costs we must live with, as it did for Moses.
  • God provides even when his servants fail. Despite Moses' anger, “water came out abundantly” (Numbers 20:11, WEB); God's mercy to a thirsty people is not undone by the failings of their leader.
  1. What exactly did God tell Moses to do, and how did Moses disobey?
  2. Why does God say Moses and Aaron failed to treat him as holy before the people?
  3. Why is the consequence, being barred from the land, so weighty even though God still gives water?
  4. What losses pile up in this chapter, and how does it mark the passing of a generation?
  5. When have you been tempted to act out of frustration rather than careful obedience, and what helps you respond well?
  1. God told Moses to speak to the rock so it would pour out water, but Moses spoke harshly to the people and struck the rock twice (20:8, 10-11). His action departed from God's specific command and was driven by anger rather than faith.
  2. By striking the rock in anger and claiming, 'shall we bring water out of this rock,' Moses obscured that the power and provision were God's (20:10-12). He failed to honor God's holiness before the watching people, a serious matter for a leader.
  3. Being barred from the land is a heavy consequence because it touched the deepest hope of Moses' life (20:12). Yet God still gives water abundantly, showing that consequence and mercy can stand together; sin is taken seriously even as grace continues.
  4. Miriam dies, Moses and Aaron fail at Meribah, Edom refuses passage, and Aaron dies on Mount Hor (20:1, 12, 21, 28). The chapter is heavy with loss, marking the steady passing of the wilderness generation even as the journey continues.
  5. This is a personal-application question. Invite members to reflect on moments when frustration has led them to act against God's clear word. As leader, encourage honesty and grace, pointing to the patience that helps us obey carefully under pressure.

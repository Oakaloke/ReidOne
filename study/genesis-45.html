Genesis 45: Joseph Reveals Himself

Joseph weeps as he makes himself known to his brothers, declaring that God sent him to preserve life.

Coming soon

Genesis 45 (WEB)

1 Then Joseph couldn’t control himself before all those who stood before him, and he cried, “Cause everyone to go out from me!” No one else stood with him, while Joseph made himself known to his brothers.

2 He wept aloud. The Egyptians heard, and the house of Pharaoh heard.

3 Joseph said to his brothers, “I am Joseph! Does my father still live?” His brothers couldn’t answer him; for they were terrified at his presence.

4 Joseph said to his brothers, “Come near to me, please.” They came near. “He said, I am Joseph, your brother, whom you sold into Egypt.

5 Now don’t be grieved, nor angry with yourselves, that you sold me here, for God sent me before you to preserve life.

6 For these two years the famine has been in the land, and there are yet five years, in which there will be neither plowing nor harvest.

7 God sent me before you to preserve for you a remnant in the earth, and to save you alive by a great deliverance.

8 So now it wasn’t you who sent me here, but God, and he has made me a father to Pharaoh, lord of all his house, and ruler over all the land of Egypt.

9 Hurry, and go up to my father, and tell him, ‘This is what your son Joseph says, “God has made me lord of all Egypt. Come down to me. Don’t wait.

10 You shall dwell in the land of Goshen, and you will be near to me, you, your children, your children’s children, your flocks, your herds, and all that you have.

11 There I will nourish you; for there are yet five years of famine; lest you come to poverty, you, and your household, and all that you have.”’

12 Behold, your eyes see, and the eyes of my brother Benjamin, that it is my mouth that speaks to you.

13 You shall tell my father of all my glory in Egypt, and of all that you have seen. You shall hurry and bring my father down here.”

14 He fell on his brother Benjamin’s neck, and wept, and Benjamin wept on his neck.

15 He kissed all his brothers, and wept on them. After that his brothers talked with him.

16 The report of it was heard in Pharaoh’s house, saying, “Joseph’s brothers have come.” It pleased Pharaoh well, and his servants.

17 Pharaoh said to Joseph, “Tell your brothers, ‘Do this. Load your animals, and go, travel to the land of Canaan.

18 Take your father and your households, and come to me, and I will give you the good of the land of Egypt, and you will eat the fat of the land.’

19 Now you are commanded: do this. Take wagons out of the land of Egypt for your little ones, and for your wives, and bring your father, and come.

20 Also, don’t concern yourselves about your belongings, for the good of all of the land of Egypt is yours.”

21 The sons of Israel did so. Joseph gave them wagons, according to the commandment of Pharaoh, and gave them provision for the way.

22 He gave each one of them changes of clothing, but to Benjamin he gave three hundred pieces of silver and five changes of clothing.

23 He sent the following to his father: ten donkeys loaded with the good things of Egypt, and ten female donkeys loaded with grain and bread and provision for his father by the way.

24 So he sent his brothers away, and they departed. He said to them, “See that you don’t quarrel on the way.”

25 They went up out of Egypt, and came into the land of Canaan, to Jacob their father.

26 They told him, saying, “Joseph is still alive, and he is ruler over all the land of Egypt.” His heart fainted, for he didn’t believe them.

27 They told him all the words of Joseph, which he had said to them. When he saw the wagons which Joseph had sent to carry him, the spirit of Jacob, their father, revived.

28 Israel said, “It is enough. Joseph my son is still alive. I will go and see him before I die.”

Summary

Unable to control himself, Joseph sends everyone out and reveals his identity to his brothers, weeping aloud. Terrified, they cannot answer, but he draws them near and tells them not to be grieved or angry with themselves, for God sent him ahead to preserve life through the famine. He urges them to hurry and bring their father down to live in Goshen, near him, where he will provide for them through the remaining five years of famine. He embraces Benjamin and kisses all his brothers. Pharaoh, pleased at the news, sends wagons and provisions and promises them the good of the land of Egypt. Joseph sends them off with gifts and a caution not to quarrel on the way. When Jacob hears that Joseph is alive and sees the wagons, his fainting heart revives and he resolves to go see his son before he dies.

Main Characters

  • Joseph — The brother who reveals himself, forgives, and interprets his suffering as God's plan to preserve life.
  • Joseph's brothers — The men who stand terrified before the one they sold, then are reconciled and sent home with good news.
  • Pharaoh — The king of Egypt who gladly welcomes Joseph's family and provides wagons and the best of the land.
  • Jacob (Israel) — The father whose heart faints, then revives, when he learns Joseph is alive and resolves to see him.

Key Verse

Genesis 45:5 (WEB)

Now don’t be grieved, nor angry with yourselves, that you sold me here, for God sent me before you to preserve life.

Lessons Learned

  • Forgiveness can transform our deepest wounds into reconciliation and welcome.
  • God can weave even our betrayals and suffering into his purpose to preserve and bless.
  • Trusting God's sovereignty frees us from being trapped in grief, guilt, and self-reproach.
  • Good news of restored relationship has power to revive a weary and despairing heart.
  • God can redirect human wrong toward his saving purpose. Joseph tells his brothers, 'God sent me before you to preserve life,' seeing God's hand behind their sin (Genesis 45:5, WEB).
  • Forgiveness releases others from condemnation. Joseph urges, 'Now don't be grieved, nor angry with yourselves,' lifting the burden of guilt from his brothers (Genesis 45:5, WEB).
  • God's sovereignty does not erase human responsibility but overrules it for good. Joseph affirms, 'it wasn't you who sent me here, but God,' trusting that God ruled over their choices (Genesis 45:8, WEB).
  • Restored relationship overflows in tears and tenderness. Joseph 'fell on his brother Benjamin's neck, and wept,' then kissed and wept over all his brothers (Genesis 45:14-15, WEB).
  • Good news can revive a despairing heart. When Jacob saw the wagons, 'the spirit of Jacob, their father, revived,' and he resolved to see his son (Genesis 45:27, WEB).
  • God's provision invites us to come and rest near him. Joseph invites his family to Goshen: 'There I will nourish you,' offering nearness and care through the famine (Genesis 45:10-11, WEB).
  1. How does Joseph reassure his brothers when they are terrified at his presence (Genesis 45:5)?
  2. What does Joseph mean when he says, 'it wasn't you who sent me here, but God' (Genesis 45:8)?
  3. Why does Jacob's heart faint at first before his spirit revives (Genesis 45:26-27)?
  4. How have you seen God bring good out of something painful in your own life?
  5. What makes it hard to forgive as Joseph does, and what might help you move toward reconciliation?
  1. Joseph tells them not to be grieved or angry with themselves, because God sent him ahead to preserve life (Genesis 45:5); he absorbs the wrong and reframes it as part of God's saving plan.
  2. Joseph does not deny that his brothers sold him, but he confesses that God's purpose stood behind and above their act, using it to position Joseph to save many lives (Genesis 45:8). Note how this holds both human responsibility and divine sovereignty together.
  3. The news is too good and too sudden to believe after years of grieving Joseph as dead (Genesis 45:26); only when he sees the tangible wagons does his spirit revive, showing how evidence steadies shaken faith.
  4. This is a personal question. Encourage members to share specific stories of redemption; some may still be waiting, so handle the question gently and avoid forcing tidy conclusions.
  5. This is a personal application question. Discuss the cost of forgiveness and what Joseph models, then help the group name practical steps toward reconciliation while acknowledging it can take time and may not be fully mutual.

John 9: The Man Born Blind Sees

Jesus heals a man blind from birth, and as the religious leaders interrogate him, the healed man grows in faith while they grow in blindness.

John 9 (WEB)

1 As he passed by, he saw a man blind from birth.

2 His disciples asked him, “Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?”

3 Jesus answered, “Neither did this man sin, nor his parents; but, that the works of God might be revealed in him.

4 I must work the works of him who sent me, while it is day. The night is coming, when no one can work.

5 While I am in the world, I am the light of the world.”

6 When he had said this, he spat on the ground, made mud with the saliva, anointed the blind man’s eyes with the mud,

7 and said to him, “Go, wash in the pool of Siloam” (which means “Sent”). So he went away, washed, and came back seeing.

8 The neighbors therefore, and those who saw that he was blind before, said, “Isn’t this he who sat and begged?”

9 Others were saying, “It is he.” Still others were saying, “He looks like him.” He said, “I am he.”

10 They therefore were asking him, “How were your eyes opened?”

11 He answered, “A man called Jesus made mud, anointed my eyes, and said to me, ‘Go to the pool of Siloam, and wash.’ So I went away and washed, and I received sight.”

12 Then they asked him, “Where is he?” He said, “I don’t know.”

13 They brought him who had been blind to the Pharisees.

14 It was a Sabbath when Jesus made the mud and opened his eyes.

15 Again therefore the Pharisees also asked him how he received his sight. He said to them, “He put mud on my eyes, I washed, and I see.”

16 Some therefore of the Pharisees said, “This man is not from God, because he doesn’t keep the Sabbath.” Others said, “How can a man who is a sinner do such signs?” There was division among them.

17 Therefore they asked the blind man again, “What do you say about him, because he opened your eyes?” He said, “He is a prophet.”

18 The Jews therefore did not believe concerning him, that he had been blind, and had received his sight, until they called the parents of him who had received his sight,

19 and asked them, “Is this your son, whom you say was born blind? How then does he now see?”

20 His parents answered them, “We know that this is our son, and that he was born blind;

21 but how he now sees, we don’t know; or who opened his eyes, we don’t know. He is of age. Ask him. He will speak for himself.”

22 His parents said these things because they feared the Jews; for the Jews had already agreed that if any man would confess him as Christ, he would be put out of the synagogue.

23 Therefore his parents said, “He is of age. Ask him.”

24 So they called the man who was blind a second time, and said to him, “Give glory to God. We know that this man is a sinner.”

25 He therefore answered, “I don’t know if he is a sinner. One thing I do know: that though I was blind, now I see.”

26 They said to him again, “What did he do to you? How did he open your eyes?”

27 He answered them, “I told you already, and you didn’t listen. Why do you want to hear it again? You don’t also want to become his disciples, do you?”

28 They insulted him and said, “You are his disciple, but we are disciples of Moses.

29 We know that God has spoken to Moses. But as for this man, we don’t know where he comes from.”

30 The man answered them, “How amazing! You don’t know where he comes from, yet he opened my eyes.

31 We know that God doesn’t listen to sinners, but if anyone is a worshiper of God, and does his will, he listens to him.

32 Since the world began it has never been heard of that anyone opened the eyes of someone born blind.

33 If this man were not from God, he could do nothing.”

34 They answered him, “You were altogether born in sins, and do you teach us?” They threw him out.

35 Jesus heard that they had thrown him out, and finding him, he said, “Do you believe in the Son of God?”

36 He answered, “Who is he, Lord, that I may believe in him?”

37 Jesus said to him, “You have both seen him, and it is he who speaks with you.”

38 He said, “Lord, I believe!” and he worshiped him.

39 Jesus said, “I came into this world for judgment, that those who don’t see may see; and that those who see may become blind.”

40 Those of the Pharisees who were with him heard these things, and said to him, “Are we also blind?”

41 Jesus said to them, “If you were blind, you would have no sin; but now you say, ‘We see.’ Therefore your sin remains.

34 They answered and said unto him, Thou wast altogether born in sins, and dost thou teach us? And they cast him out.

35 Jesus heard that they had cast him out; and when he had found him, he said unto him, Dost thou believe on the Son of God?

36 He answered and said, Who is he, Lord, that I might believe on him?

38 And he said, Lord, I believe. And he worshipped him.

39 And Jesus said, For judgment I am come into this world, that they which see not might see; and that they which see might be made blind.

40 And some of the Pharisees which were with him heard these words, and said unto him, Are we blind also?

41 Jesus said unto them, If ye were blind, ye should have no sin: but now ye say, We see; therefore your sin remaineth.

Summary

Passing by, Jesus sees a man blind from birth. His disciples ask whose sin caused it, but Jesus says it happened so the works of God might be displayed in him. He makes mud with saliva, anoints the man's eyes, and tells him to wash in the pool of Siloam; the man comes back seeing. Neighbors are amazed, and the Pharisees investigate because the healing happened on the Sabbath. They question the man repeatedly and summon his parents, who fear being put out of the synagogue. The healed man boldly insists that Jesus is from God, and the leaders cast him out. Jesus finds him, reveals himself as the Son of Man, and the man worships him. Jesus says he came so that the blind may see and those who claim to see may be shown blind.

Main Characters

  • Jesus — Heals the man born blind and reveals himself as the Son of Man and the light of the world.
  • The man born blind — Healed by Jesus, he grows from confusion to bold testimony to worship.
  • The Pharisees — Investigate the healing, reject Jesus, and cast out the man who confesses him.
  • The man's parents — Confirm their son was born blind but fear the leaders and deflect questions about Jesus.

Key Verse

John 9:25 (WEB)

He therefore answered, “I don’t know if he is a sinner. One thing I do know: that though I was blind, now I see.”

Lessons Learned

  • God can turn our suffering into a stage for displaying his glory and works.
  • Genuine faith grows as we hold to what Christ has done, even under pressure.
  • Fear of people can keep us from confessing the truth we know about Jesus.
  • Spiritual blindness is most dangerous in those who are sure they already see.
  • God works his purposes even through hardship. Jesus says the man's blindness is not from sin, but 'that the works of God might be revealed in him' (John 9:3, WEB).
  • An encounter with Christ is undeniable. The healed man testifies, 'One thing I do know: that though I was blind, now I see' (John 9:25, WEB), holding to his experience of Jesus.
  • Fear of others can silence true confession. His parents deflect the questions 'because they feared the Jews' and the threat of being put out of the synagogue (John 9:22, WEB).
  • Christ seeks out those rejected for his name. After the man is cast out, Jesus finds him and leads him to worship, asking, 'Do you believe in the Son of God?' (John 9:35, WEB).
  • Claiming to see while rejecting Christ is the deepest blindness. Jesus says, 'For judgment I came into this world, that those who don't see may see; and that those who see may become blind' (John 9:39, WEB).
  1. How does Jesus reframe the disciples' question about whose sin caused the blindness?
  2. How does the healed man's understanding of Jesus deepen through his repeated questioning?
  3. Why do the man's parents avoid answering, and what does their fear reveal?
  4. What does Jesus mean about the blind seeing and the seeing becoming blind, and how do the Pharisees fit this?
  5. Where in your own life is Jesus inviting you to confess what he has done, even when it is costly?
  1. Rather than assigning blame, Jesus says the blindness is an occasion for God's works to be revealed (John 9:3). He shifts the focus from explaining suffering to trusting God's purpose, then heals the man through the pool of Siloam (9:6-7).
  2. At first the man calls his healer 'the man called Jesus' (9:11), then 'a prophet' (9:17), then insists he must be from God (9:33). Finally, meeting Jesus, he confesses 'Lord, I believe' and worships him (9:38), a picture of growing faith.
  3. The parents confirm he was born blind but say to ask their grown son about how he sees, because they feared being put out of the synagogue (John 9:20-22). Their fear shows how social pressure can keep people from confessing Christ.
  4. Jesus came so the spiritually blind would receive sight and the self-assured would be exposed (John 9:39). The Pharisees, confident they see, refuse the light and so remain in their sin (9:40-41), the very blindness they deny.
  5. This is a personal application question. Invite members to consider where confessing Christ might cost them comfort or approval, like the healed man. As leader, encourage honest reflection and assure the group no one will be pressed to share more than they wish.

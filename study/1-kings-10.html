1 Kings 10: The Queen and the Glory

The queen of Sheba comes to test Solomon's wisdom and is left breathless, as the king's fame, gold, and splendor reach their dazzling height.

Coming soon

1 Kings 10 (WEB)

1 When the queen of Sheba heard of the fame of Solomon concerning Yahweh’s name, she came to prove him with hard questions.

2 She came to Jerusalem with a very great train, with camels that bore spices, and very much gold, and precious stones; and when she had come to Solomon, she talked with him of all that was in her heart.

3 Solomon told her all her questions: there was not anything hidden from the king which he didn’t tell her.

4 When the queen of Sheba had seen all the wisdom of Solomon, and the house that he had built,

5 and the food of his table, and the sitting of his servants, and the attendance of his ministers, and their clothing, and his cup bearers, and his ascent by which he went up to Yahweh’s house; there was no more spirit in her.

6 She said to the king, “It was a true report that I heard in my own land of your acts, and of your wisdom.

7 However I didn’t believe the words, until I came, and my eyes had seen it. Behold, the half was not told me! Your wisdom and prosperity exceed the fame which I heard.

8 Happy are your men, happy are these your servants, who stand continually before you, who hear your wisdom.

9 Blessed is Yahweh your God, who delighted in you, to set you on the throne of Israel. Because Yahweh loved Israel forever, therefore made he you king, to do justice and righteousness.”

10 She gave the king one hundred twenty talents of gold, and of spices very great store, and precious stones. There came no more such abundance of spices as these which the queen of Sheba gave to king Solomon.

11 The navy also of Hiram, that brought gold from Ophir, brought in from Ophir great plenty of almug trees and precious stones.

12 The king made of the almug trees pillars for Yahweh’s house, and for the king’s house, harps also and stringed instruments for the singers: there came no such almug trees, nor were seen, to this day.

13 King Solomon gave to the queen of Sheba all her desire, whatever she asked, besides that which Solomon gave her of his royal bounty. So she turned, and went to her own land, she and her servants.

14 Now the weight of gold that came to Solomon in one year was six hundred sixty-six talents of gold,

15 besides that which the traders brought, and the traffic of the merchants, and of all the kings of the mixed people, and of the governors of the country.

16 King Solomon made two hundred bucklers of beaten gold; six hundred shekels of gold went to one buckler.

17 he made three hundred shields of beaten gold; three minas of gold went to one shield: and the king put them in the house of the forest of Lebanon.

18 Moreover the king made a great throne of ivory, and overlaid it with the finest gold.

19 There were six steps to the throne, and the top of the throne was round behind; and there were stays on either side by the place of the seat, and two lions standing beside the stays.

20 Twelve lions stood there on the one side and on the other on the six steps: there was nothing like it made in any kingdom.

21 All king Solomon’s drinking vessels were of gold, and all the vessels of the house of the forest of Lebanon were of pure gold: none were of silver; it was nothing accounted of in the days of Solomon.

22 For the king had at sea a navy of Tarshish with the navy of Hiram: once every three years came the navy of Tarshish, bringing gold, and silver, ivory, and apes, and peacocks.

23 So king Solomon exceeded all the kings of the earth in riches and in wisdom.

24 All the earth sought the presence of Solomon, to hear his wisdom, which God had put in his heart.

25 They brought every man his tribute, vessels of silver, and vessels of gold, and clothing, and armor, and spices, horses, and mules, a rate year by year.

26 Solomon gathered together chariots and horsemen: and he had a thousand and four hundred chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen, that he bestowed in the chariot cities, and with the king at Jerusalem.

27 The king made silver to be in Jerusalem as stones, and cedars made he to be as the sycamore trees that are in the lowland, for abundance.

28 The horses which Solomon had were brought out of Egypt; and the king’s merchants received them in droves, each drove at a price.

29 A chariot came up and went out of Egypt for six hundred shekels of silver, and a horse for one hundred fifty; and so for all the kings of the Hittites, and for the kings of Syria, they brought them out by their means.

Summary

Drawn by Solomon's fame in connection with the name of Yahweh, the queen of Sheba travels to Jerusalem with a great caravan of spices, gold, and precious stones to test him with hard questions. Solomon answers everything, hiding nothing from her, and when she sees his wisdom, his palace, the food of his table, the bearing of his servants, and his ascent to the house of the Lord, there is no more spirit left in her. She confesses that the report she heard did not capture the half of it, blesses Solomon's men who daily hear his wisdom, and blesses Yahweh who delighted in Solomon and set him on the throne to do justice and righteousness. She gives lavish gifts, and the king gives generously in return. The chapter then catalogs Solomon's staggering wealth: six hundred sixty-six talents of gold in a single year, shields of beaten gold, a great ivory throne overlaid with gold and flanked by lions, drinking vessels all of gold, and ships bringing gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks. Silver becomes as common as stones in Jerusalem. Solomon exceeds all the kings of the earth in riches and wisdom, and the whole earth seeks his presence. Yet woven into the splendor are quiet hints—horses from Egypt, the accumulation of gold—that the king is beginning to drift from the path God set before kings.

Main Characters

  • Solomon — The king whose wisdom and splendor reach their peak, answering every question and exceeding all the kings of the earth in riches and renown.
  • The queen of Sheba — A foreign monarch who comes to test Solomon, is overwhelmed by his wisdom and glory, and blesses the Lord who set him on Israel's throne.
  • Yahweh (the LORD) — The God whom the queen blesses for loving Israel and setting Solomon on the throne to do justice and righteousness, the true source of Solomon's gifts.

Key Verse

1 Kings 10:9 (WEB)

Blessed is Yahweh your God, who delighted in you, to set you on the throne of Israel. Because Yahweh loved Israel forever, therefore made he you king, to do justice and righteousness.”

Lessons Learned

  • God-given wisdom can draw even foreign rulers to acknowledge and bless the Lord.
  • The blessings we enjoy are gifts of God's love, meant to lead us and others to praise him.
  • Earthly splendor, however dazzling, can never fully satisfy or capture the glory of God.
  • Even at the peak of success, subtle compromises can begin to pull the heart from God.
  • Wisdom from God testifies to him. The queen comes because of Solomon's fame “concerning Yahweh’s name” (1 Kings 10:1, WEB). God-given gifts point beyond the gifted one to the Giver.
  • Blessings should lead to praise. The queen blesses “Yahweh your God, who delighted in you, to set you on the throne” (1 Kings 10:9, WEB). Rightly received, abundance turns the heart to worship the Lord.
  • Splendor cannot exceed God's glory. “The half was not told me” (1 Kings 10:7, WEB) of Solomon; yet a greater than Solomon is here (Matthew 12:42). All earthly grandeur only hints at the glory of Christ.
  • Prosperity carries quiet dangers. Gold pours in and “the king made silver to be in Jerusalem as stones” (1 Kings 10:27, WEB), with horses from Egypt. The very splendor sows seeds of the drift that chapter 11 lays bare.
  1. Why does the queen of Sheba come to Solomon, and what is she seeking?
  2. How does she respond after seeing Solomon's wisdom and glory, and whom does she ultimately bless?
  3. What does the catalog of Solomon's wealth communicate, and what hints of danger lie within it?
  4. Jesus calls himself one “greater than Solomon” (Matthew 12:42). How does Solomon's splendor point us to Christ?
  5. The queen's longing was satisfied beyond her expectations. Where do you look for satisfaction, and how does Christ surpass every earthly glory?
  1. The queen travels far, drawn by Solomon's fame linked to Yahweh's name, to test him with hard questions (10:1). She seeks wisdom and truth, and her long journey shows how God's gift to Solomon was drawing the nations toward the God of Israel.
  2. Overwhelmed until there is no spirit left in her, she confesses the half was not told her and then blesses Yahweh for loving Israel and setting Solomon on the throne (10:5-9). Her praise rightly moves past the king to the God who gave him wisdom and rule.
  3. The lists of gold, the ivory throne, and golden vessels display unmatched wealth and wisdom (10:14-25). Yet the influx of gold, the horses from Egypt, and the accumulation hint at the very excesses kings were warned against, foreshadowing Solomon's coming decline.
  4. Solomon's wisdom and glory drew the nations and pointed to God, but Jesus is the greater King whose wisdom and reign are perfect and eternal (Matthew 12:42). Solomon's splendor, real yet fading, awakens longing for the King whose kingdom and glory never end.
  5. This is a personal-application question. Invite members to examine where they seek lasting satisfaction and to consider how Christ, greater than Solomon, alone fully satisfies the heart. As leader, encourage gentle honesty and turn the group's longing toward the surpassing worth of Jesus.

1 Corinthians 6: Bought With a Price

Paul rebukes believers for suing one another and for sexual sin, reminding them their bodies belong to the Lord and house the Spirit.

1 Corinthians 6 (WEB)

1 Dare any of you, having a matter against his neighbor, go to law before the unrighteous, and not before the saints?

2 Don’t you know that the saints will judge the world? And if the world is judged by you, are you unworthy to judge the smallest matters?

3 Don’t you know that we will judge angels? How much more, things that pertain to this life?

4 If then, you have to judge things pertaining to this life, do you set them to judge who are of no account in the assembly?

5 I say this to move you to shame. Isn’t there even one wise man among you who would be able to decide between his brothers?

6 But brother goes to law with brother, and that before unbelievers!

7 Therefore it is already altogether a defect in you, that you have lawsuits one with another. Why not rather be wronged? Why not rather be defrauded?

8 No, but you yourselves do wrong, and defraud, and that against your brothers.

9 Or don’t you know that the unrighteous will not inherit the Kingdom of God? Don’t be deceived. Neither the sexually immoral, nor idolaters, nor adulterers, nor male prostitutes, nor homosexuals,

10 nor thieves, nor covetous, nor drunkards, nor slanderers, nor extortionists, will inherit the Kingdom of God.

11 Such were some of you, but you were washed. But you were sanctified. But you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and in the Spirit of our God.

12 “All things are lawful for me,” but not all things are expedient. “All things are lawful for me,” but I will not be brought under the power of anything.

13 “Foods for the belly, and the belly for foods,” but God will bring to nothing both it and them. But the body is not for sexual immorality, but for the Lord; and the Lord for the body.

14 Now God raised up the Lord, and will also raise us up by his power.

15 Don’t you know that your bodies are members of Christ? Shall I then take the members of Christ, and make them members of a prostitute? May it never be!

16 Or don’t you know that he who is joined to a prostitute is one body? For, “The two”, he says, “will become one flesh.”

17 But he who is joined to the Lord is one spirit.

18 Flee sexual immorality! “Every sin that a man does is outside the body,” but he who commits sexual immorality sins against his own body.

19 Or don’t you know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit which is in you, which you have from God? You are not your own,

20 for you were bought with a price. Therefore glorify God in your body and in your spirit, which are God’s.

Summary

Paul is dismayed that believers are dragging one another before pagan courts. Do they not know that the saints will one day judge the world, even angels? Surely they can settle ordinary matters among themselves; it would be better to be wronged or defrauded than to sue a brother before unbelievers. In fact, to defraud one another is itself a serious failure. Then Paul issues a sober warning: the unrighteous will not inherit the kingdom of God. He lists the sins that mark such a life—immorality, idolatry, theft, greed, and more—and then adds the gospel turn: such were some of you, but you were washed, sanctified, and justified in the name of the Lord Jesus and by the Spirit of God. Taking up the Corinthians' own slogan, all things are lawful for me, Paul insists that not all things are helpful and that he will not be mastered by anything. The body is not for sexual immorality but for the Lord, and the Lord for the body; God raised the Lord and will raise us too. Believers' bodies are members of Christ, so to join oneself to a prostitute is unthinkable, for the two become one flesh, while the one joined to the Lord is one spirit with him. Therefore flee sexual immorality, the one sin against one's own body. The body is a temple of the Holy Spirit; we are not our own, for we were bought with a price, and so must glorify God in our bodies.

Main Characters

  • Paul — The apostle who confronts both lawsuits and sexual sin, grounding his appeal in the gospel and the dignity of the redeemed body.
  • The litigious believers — Members who take one another to pagan courts, when they should rather suffer wrong or settle disputes within the church.
  • The Holy Spirit — The Spirit of God who indwells the believer's body as a temple, making sexual holiness a matter of sacred honor.
  • Christ the Redeemer — The Lord who washed, sanctified, and justified the Corinthians and bought them at a price so they belong to him, body and spirit.

Lessons Learned

  • Believers should settle disputes within the church rather than before unbelievers.
  • Suffering wrong is better than wronging a brother and dishonoring Christ.
  • The gospel transforms us—we were washed, sanctified, and justified by grace.
  • Our bodies belong to the Lord and are temples of the Spirit, to be kept holy.
  • Be willing to be wronged. “Why not rather be wronged? Why not rather be defrauded?” (1 Corinthians 6:7, WEB). The gospel makes us willing to lose for the sake of love and witness.
  • Grace changes what we were. “Such were some of you, but you were washed” (1 Corinthians 6:11, WEB). No past sin is beyond the cleansing of Christ.
  • Flee sexual immorality. “Flee sexual immorality!” (1 Corinthians 6:18, WEB). Some sins are to be resisted by running, not reasoning.
  • You were bought with a price. “For you were bought with a price” (1 Corinthians 6:20, WEB). We belong to God, so we glorify him in our bodies.
  1. Why does Paul say it is shameful for believers to sue one another before unbelievers?
  2. What does it mean that it is better to be wronged than to wrong a brother?
  3. How does the phrase such were some of you offer hope to anyone weighed down by their past?
  4. Why does Paul ground sexual holiness in the truth that our bodies are temples of the Spirit?
  5. What does it look like in your own life to glorify God in your body because you are not your own?
  1. Public lawsuits between believers air the family's quarrels before a watching world and deny the wisdom God has given the church (6:1-6). They reveal a failure of love and a poor witness, when the gospel calls for reconciliation among the saints.
  2. Insisting on every right, even at a brother's expense, contradicts the way of Christ, who suffered wrong for us (6:7-8). Sometimes the most Christlike response is to absorb loss rather than to win and wound the body.
  3. The list of sins is followed by a glorious but: they were washed, sanctified, and justified (6:11). This assures anyone burdened by the past that the gospel does not merely forgive but transforms, making sinners into saints.
  4. Because the Spirit dwells in us and we were purchased by Christ, the body is not a neutral instrument but holy ground (6:19-20). Sexual sin profanes a temple and denies that we belong, body and soul, to the Lord.
  5. This is a personal-application question. Invite members to consider, gently and without shame, how the truth of being bought with a price reshapes their use of time, appetites, and bodies. As leader, keep the focus on belonging to a loving Redeemer rather than mere rule-keeping.

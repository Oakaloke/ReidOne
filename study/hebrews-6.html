Hebrews 6: An Anchor for the Soul

After a severe warning against falling away, the writer rests his readers' hope on God's unbreakable oath and Jesus, our forerunner within the veil.

Coming soon

Hebrews 6 (WEB)

1 Therefore leaving the teaching of the first principles of Christ, let us press on to perfection—not laying again a foundation of repentance from dead works, of faith toward God,

2 of the teaching of baptisms, of laying on of hands, of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment.

3 This will we do, if God permits.

4 For concerning those who were once enlightened and tasted of the heavenly gift, and were made partakers of the Holy Spirit,

5 and tasted the good word of God, and the powers of the age to come,

6 and then fell away, it is impossible to renew them again to repentance; seeing they crucify the Son of God for themselves again, and put him to open shame.

7 For the land which has drunk the rain that comes often on it, and produces a crop suitable for them for whose sake it is also tilled, receives blessing from God;

8 but if it bears thorns and thistles, it is rejected and near being cursed, whose end is to be burned.

9 But, beloved, we are persuaded of better things for you, and things that accompany salvation, even though we speak like this.

10 For God is not unrighteous, so as to forget your work and the labor of love which you showed toward his name, in that you served the saints, and still do serve them.

11 We desire that each one of you may show the same diligence to the fullness of hope even to the end,

12 that you won’t be sluggish, but imitators of those who through faith and patience inherited the promises.

13 For when God made a promise to Abraham, since he could swear by no one greater, he swore by himself,

14 saying, “Surely blessing I will bless you, and multiplying I will multiply you.”

15 Thus, having patiently endured, he obtained the promise.

16 For men indeed swear by a greater one, and in every dispute of theirs the oath is final for confirmation.

17 In this way God, being determined to show more abundantly to the heirs of the promise the immutability of his counsel, interposed with an oath;

18 that by two immutable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie, we may have a strong encouragement, who have fled for refuge to take hold of the hope set before us.

19 This hope we have as an anchor of the soul, a hope both sure and steadfast and entering into that which is within the veil;

20 where as a forerunner Jesus entered for us, having become a high priest forever after the order of Melchizedek.

Hebrews 6 (KJV)

1 Therefore leaving the principles of the doctrine of Christ, let us go on unto perfection; not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works, and of faith toward God,

2 Of the doctrine of baptisms, and of laying on of hands, and of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment.

3 And this will we do, if God permit.

4 For it is impossible for those who were once enlightened, and have tasted of the heavenly gift, and were made partakers of the Holy Ghost,

5 And have tasted the good word of God, and the powers of the world to come,

6 If they shall fall away, to renew them again unto repentance; seeing they crucify to themselves the Son of God afresh, and put him to an open shame.

7 For the earth which drinketh in the rain that cometh oft upon it, and bringeth forth herbs meet for them by whom it is dressed, receiveth blessing from God:

8 But that which beareth thorns and briers is rejected, and is nigh unto cursing; whose end is to be burned.

9 But, beloved, we are persuaded better things of you, and things that accompany salvation, though we thus speak.

10 For God is not unrighteous to forget your work and labour of love, which ye have shewed toward his name, in that ye have ministered to the saints, and do minister.

11 And we desire that every one of you do shew the same diligence to the full assurance of hope unto the end:

12 That ye be not slothful, but followers of them who through faith and patience inherit the promises.

13 For when God made promise to Abraham, because he could swear by no greater, he sware by himself,

14 Saying, Surely blessing I will bless thee, and multiplying I will multiply thee.

15 And so, after he had patiently endured, he obtained the promise.

16 For men verily swear by the greater: and an oath for confirmation is to them an end of all strife.

17 Wherein God, willing more abundantly to shew unto the heirs of promise the immutability of his counsel, confirmed it by an oath:

18 That by two immutable things, in which it was impossible for God to lie, we might have a strong consolation, who have fled for refuge to lay hold upon the hope set before us:

19 Which hope we have as an anchor of the soul, both sure and stedfast, and which entereth into that within the veil;

20 Whither the forerunner is for us entered, even Jesus, made an high priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec.

Summary

Urging his readers to press on toward maturity rather than relay the foundation again, the writer lists elementary truths he wants to move beyond and then sounds his sharpest warning. Those who were once enlightened, tasted the heavenly gift, shared in the Holy Spirit, and tasted God's good word and the powers of the coming age, yet then fall away, cannot be renewed again to repentance, for they crucify the Son of God afresh and hold him up to contempt. Like land that drinks the rain, ground that bears a useful crop receives blessing, but ground that produces thorns is worthless and near to being cursed and burned. Yet the writer immediately reassures his beloved readers that he is confident of better things from them, things that accompany salvation, for God is not unjust and will not forget their work and love shown to his name in serving the saints. He longs for each of them to show the same diligence to the very end, imitating those who through faith and patience inherit the promises. To ground their assurance, he points to Abraham: God made a promise and, having no one greater to swear by, swore by himself, and Abraham patiently waited and obtained it. Since God confirmed his promise with an oath, two unchangeable things—his promise and his oath, in which it is impossible for God to lie—give strong encouragement to those who have fled for refuge. This hope is a sure and steadfast anchor of the soul, reaching within the veil, where Jesus has entered as our forerunner, having become a high priest forever after the order of Melchizedek.

Key Figures

  • God who cannot lie — The one who confirmed his promise to Abraham with an oath, giving heirs of the promise two unchangeable things as strong encouragement for hope.
  • Abraham — The patriarch who, having patiently endured, obtained the promise, held up as the model of faith and patience that inherits God's word.
  • Jesus the forerunner — The high priest forever after Melchizedek who has entered within the veil ahead of us, anchoring our hope in the very presence of God.

Key Verse

Hebrews 6:19 (WEB)

This hope we have as an anchor of the soul, a hope both sure and steadfast and entering into that which is within the veil;

Lessons Learned

  • Genuine faith presses on to maturity rather than endlessly relaying the foundation.
  • Turning away after tasting the things of God is a grave and tragic danger.
  • God remembers and values the love and service his people show to his name.
  • Our hope is anchored in God's unchangeable promise and oath, secured by Jesus in heaven.
  • Press on, don't merely repeat. “Leaving the teaching of the first principles… let us press on to perfection” (Hebrews 6:1, WEB). Spiritual life is meant to advance, not stall at the basics.
  • God notices loving service. “God is not unrighteous, so as to forget your work and the labor of love” (Hebrews 6:10, WEB). No quiet act of service for his name is overlooked.
  • God's promise is doubly sure. By “two immutable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie” (Hebrews 6:18, WEB), we have strong encouragement. His word and his oath cannot fail.
  • Hope is an anchor that holds in heaven. Our hope reaches “within the veil” where Jesus has gone as forerunner (Hebrews 6:19-20, WEB). The anchor is fixed not in our feelings but in the presence of God.
  1. Why does the writer urge his readers to press on rather than relay the foundation again (6:1-3)?
  2. How should we understand the solemn warning against falling away in 6:4-6?
  3. What grounds for confidence does the writer give his readers right after the warning (6:9-10)?
  4. Why does God confirm his promise with an oath, and what comfort does that give (6:13-18)?
  5. What does it mean for you that hope is an “anchor of the soul” reaching where Jesus has already gone (6:19-20)?
  1. The readers' immaturity (ch. 5) keeps them spiritually fragile, so the writer presses them toward growth as the path of safety. Constantly re-laying the foundation never builds a house. Help the group see that pressing on in faith is itself part of how God preserves us.
  2. This sober passage describes those who have shared deeply in spiritual experiences yet finally turn away, treating Christ with contempt. Christians have long debated its exact reach; the pastoral point is clear and urgent—do not turn back. Note that the very next verses express confidence that the readers will persevere, so the warning serves to keep them, not to crush them.
  3. He is “persuaded of better things” for them, evidenced by their love and service to the saints, which God will not forget (6:9-10). The warning is balanced by encouragement and the assurance that real fruit accompanies salvation. This models how to challenge and reassure at once.
  4. Though God's promise alone is utterly reliable, he condescends to swear an oath so that we have doubled assurance (6:13-18). Since it is impossible for God to lie, our hope rests on bedrock. Encourage the group to anchor their assurance in God's character, not their fluctuating feelings.
  5. The image is of a soul tethered to the unshakable presence of God, where Jesus our forerunner has already gone ahead (6:19-20). Storms may toss us, but the anchor holds in heaven itself. This is a personal-application question; invite members to name what their hope is truly anchored to.

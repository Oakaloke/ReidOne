1 Kings 9: A Covenant Warning

God appears again to Solomon, promising to establish his throne if he stays faithful but warning of judgment and exile if Israel turns to other gods.

1 Kings 9 (WEB)

1 When Solomon had finished the building of Yahweh’s house, and the king’s house, and all Solomon’s desire which he was pleased to do,

2 Yahweh appeared to Solomon the second time, as he had appeared to him at Gibeon.

3 Yahweh said to him, “I have heard your prayer and your supplication, that you have made before me. I have made this house holy, which you have built, to put my name there forever; and my eyes and my heart shall be there perpetually.

4 As for you, if you will walk before me, as David your father walked, in integrity of heart, and in uprightness, to do according to all that I have commanded you, and will keep my statutes and my ordinances;

5 then I will establish the throne of your kingdom over Israel forever, according as I promised to David your father, saying, ‘There shall not fail you a man on the throne of Israel.’

6 But if you turn away from following me, you or your children, and not keep my commandments and my statutes which I have set before you, but shall go and serve other gods, and worship them;

7 then I will cut off Israel out of the land which I have given them; and this house, which I have made holy for my name, will I cast out of my sight; and Israel shall be a proverb and a byword among all peoples.

8 Though this house is so high, yet shall everyone who passes by it be astonished, and shall hiss; and they shall say, ‘Why has Yahweh done thus to this land, and to this house?’

9 and they shall answer, ‘Because they abandoned Yahweh their God, who brought their fathers out of the land of Egypt, and laid hold of other gods, and worshiped them, and served them. Therefore Yahweh has brought all this evil on them.’”

10 At the end of twenty years, in which Solomon had built the two houses, Yahweh’s house and the king’s house

11 (now Hiram the king of Tyre had furnished Solomon with cedar trees and fir trees, and with gold, according to all his desire), King Solomon gave Hiram twenty cities in the land of Galilee.

12 Hiram came out of Tyre to see the cities which Solomon had given him; and they didn’t please him.

13 He said, “What cities are these which you have given me, my brother?” He called them the land of Cabul to this day.

14 Hiram sent to the king one hundred twenty talents of gold.

15 This is the reason of the levy which king Solomon raised, to build Yahweh’s house, and his own house, and Millo, and the wall of Jerusalem, and Hazor, and Megiddo, and Gezer.

16 Pharaoh king of Egypt had gone up, and taken Gezer, and burnt it with fire, and slain the Canaanites who lived in the city, and given it for a portion to his daughter, Solomon’s wife.

17 Solomon built Gezer, and Beth Horon the lower,

18 and Baalath, and Tamar in the wilderness, in the land,

19 and all the storage cities that Solomon had, and the cities for his chariots, and the cities for his horsemen, and that which Solomon desired to build for his pleasure in Jerusalem, and in Lebanon, and in all the land of his dominion.

20 As for all the people who were left of the Amorites, the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites, who were not of the children of Israel;

21 their children who were left after them in the land, whom the children of Israel were not able utterly to destroy, of them Solomon raised a levy of bondservants to this day.

22 But of the children of Israel Solomon made no bondservants; but they were the men of war, and his servants, and his princes, and his captains, and rulers of his chariots and of his horsemen.

23 These were the chief officers who were over Solomon’s work, five hundred fifty, who bore rule over the people who labored in the work.

24 But Pharaoh’s daughter came up out of the city of David to her house which Solomon had built for her: then he built Millo.

25 Solomon offered burnt offerings and peace offerings on the altar which he built to Yahweh three times a year, burning incense with them, on the altar that was before Yahweh. So he finished the house.

26 King Solomon made a navy of ships in Ezion Geber, which is beside Eloth, on the shore of the Red Sea, in the land of Edom.

27 Hiram sent in the navy his servants, sailors who had knowledge of the sea, with the servants of Solomon.

28 They came to Ophir, and fetched from there gold, four hundred and twenty talents, and brought it to king Solomon.

Summary

When Solomon has finished the temple and his palace, Yahweh appears to him a second time, as he had at Gibeon. God assures Solomon that he has heard his prayer and consecrated the house, putting his name there forever, with his eyes and heart on it perpetually. Then comes the heart of the chapter: a solemn covenant promise and warning. If Solomon walks before God with integrity, as David did, keeping his statutes, God will establish his throne over Israel forever. But if he or his children turn away to serve other gods, God will cut Israel off from the land and cast the house out of his sight, so that the once-glorious temple becomes a heap that astonishes passersby, who will recognize it as the consequence of abandoning the Lord. The chapter then recounts Solomon's wider activity: his dealings with Hiram, the cities he gave and received, his building of fortified cities, store cities, and chariot cities, the labor force he raised from the surviving Canaanite peoples, his regular offerings on the altar three times a year, and the navy he built with Hiram's help to bring gold from Ophir. The splendor is real, but it stands under the shadow of the warning: covenant blessing is bound to covenant faithfulness.

Main Characters

  • Solomon — The king who receives God's second appearing, with its promise and warning, and who continues vast building projects, trade, and worship.
  • Yahweh (the LORD) / God — The God who consecrates the temple, promises to establish Solomon's throne if he is faithful, and warns of exile and ruin if Israel turns to idols.
  • Hiram, king of Tyre — Solomon's trading partner who supplies gold and ships, receives cities he finds displeasing, and joins Solomon's navy to bring gold from Ophir.

Key Verse

1 Kings 9:4 (WEB)

As for you, if you will walk before me, as David your father walked, in integrity of heart, and in uprightness, to do according to all that I have commanded you, and will keep my statutes and my ordinances;

Lessons Learned

  • God graciously confirms his promises, but covenant blessing is bound to covenant faithfulness.
  • Integrity of heart, not merely outward success, is what God looks for in his people.
  • Turning to other gods leads to ruin, however glorious our achievements may appear.
  • Outward splendor is no substitute for a heart that walks before God in uprightness.
  • God seeks integrity of heart. God's condition is that Solomon “walk before me, as David your father walked, in integrity of heart” (1 Kings 9:4, WEB). The Lord looks past achievements to the heart's faithfulness.
  • Faithfulness secures the promise. If Solomon obeys, “then I will establish the throne of your kingdom over Israel forever” (1 Kings 9:5, WEB). God's promises are sure, and they call forth obedient trust.
  • Idolatry brings ruin. If they “go and serve other gods,” God will cast off the house and land (1 Kings 9:6-7, WEB). Departing from the Lord undoes even the most glorious blessings.
  • Splendor is not the same as faithfulness. Amid cities, fleets, and gold from Ophir (1 Kings 9:26-28, WEB), the warning still stands. Outward greatness cannot replace a heart wholly devoted to God.
  1. Why does God appear to Solomon a second time, and what does he affirm about the temple?
  2. What is the condition attached to God's promise, and what does it emphasize about Solomon's walk?
  3. What sobering future does God describe if Israel turns to other gods?
  4. How does the account of Solomon's building and trade sit alongside the covenant warning?
  5. God called Solomon to walk before him with integrity of heart. In what area is God calling you to greater faithfulness, whatever your outward circumstances?
  1. God appears as he had at Gibeon to assure Solomon that his prayer is heard and the house is consecrated with God's name and ongoing attention (9:2-3). The appearing both encourages Solomon and sets the stage for the solemn promise and warning that follow.
  2. The condition is that Solomon walk before God in integrity and uprightness, keeping his statutes (9:4). It emphasizes inward faithfulness and obedience, not merely religious activity, as the basis on which the throne would be established.
  3. God warns that if they serve other gods, he will cut Israel off, cast out the temple, and make it a byword, so that onlookers recognize it as judgment for abandoning the Lord (9:6-9). It foreshadows the exile and underlines the seriousness of covenant unfaithfulness.
  4. The list of cities, labor, offerings, and fleets shows Solomon's height of power and wealth (9:10-28). Placed right after the warning, it quietly reminds us that all this splendor stands under the condition of faithfulness, and impressive achievements cannot secure God's blessing apart from a faithful heart.
  5. This is a personal-application question. Invite members to consider where, regardless of success or struggle, God is calling them to walk with integrity of heart. As leader, encourage honest reflection and point to Christ, whose perfect faithfulness secures the lasting kingdom we could not.

1 Samuel 19: Hunted but Kept

Saul's resolve to kill David grows open, but Jonathan, Michal, and even the Spirit of God repeatedly shield the Lord's anointed.

1 Samuel 19 (WEB)

1 Saul spoke to Jonathan his son, and to all his servants, that they should kill David. But Jonathan, Saul’s son, delighted much in David.

2 Jonathan told David, saying, “Saul my father seeks to kill you. Now therefore, please take care of yourself in the morning, and live in a secret place, and hide yourself.

3 I will go out and stand beside my father in the field where you are, and I will talk with my father about you; and if I see anything, I will tell you.”

4 Jonathan spoke good of David to Saul his father, and said to him, “Don’t let the king sin against his servant, against David; because he has not sinned against you, and because his works have been very good toward you;

5 for he put his life in his hand, and struck the Philistine, and Yahweh worked a great victory for all Israel. You saw it, and rejoiced. Why then will you sin against innocent blood, to kill David without a cause?”

6 Saul listened to the voice of Jonathan: and Saul swore, “As Yahweh lives, he shall not be put to death.”

7 Jonathan called David, and Jonathan showed him all those things. Jonathan brought David to Saul, and he was in his presence, as before.

8 There was war again. David went out, and fought with the Philistines, and killed them with a great slaughter; and they fled before him.

9 An evil spirit from Yahweh was on Saul, as he sat in his house with his spear in his hand; and David was playing with his hand.

10 Saul sought to pin David even to the wall with the spear; but he slipped away out of Saul’s presence, and he stuck the spear into the wall. David fled, and escaped that night.

11 Saul sent messengers to David’s house, to watch him, and to kill him in the morning. Michal, David’s wife, told him, saying, “If you don’t save your life tonight, tomorrow you will be killed.”

12 So Michal let David down through the window. He went, fled, and escaped.

13 Michal took the teraphim, and laid it in the bed, and put a pillow of goats’ hair at its head, and covered it with the clothes.

14 When Saul sent messengers to take David, she said, “He is sick.”

15 Saul sent the messengers to see David, saying, “Bring him up to me in the bed, that I may kill him.”

16 When the messengers came in, behold, the teraphim was in the bed, with the pillow of goats’ hair at its head.

17 Saul said to Michal, “Why have you deceived me thus, and let my enemy go, so that he is escaped?” Michal answered Saul, “He said to me, ‘Let me go! Why should I kill you?’”

18 Now David fled, and escaped, and came to Samuel to Ramah, and told him all that Saul had done to him. He and Samuel went and lived in Naioth.

19 It was told Saul, saying, “Behold, David is at Naioth in Ramah.”

20 Saul sent messengers to take David: and when they saw the company of the prophets prophesying, and Samuel standing as head over them, the Spirit of God came on the messengers of Saul, and they also prophesied.

21 When it was told Saul, he sent other messengers, and they also prophesied. Saul sent messengers again the third time, and they also prophesied.

22 Then went he also to Ramah, and came to the great well that is in Secu: and he asked, “Where are Samuel and David?” One said, “Behold, they are at Naioth in Ramah.”

23 He went there to Naioth in Ramah. Then the Spirit of God came on him also, and he went on, and prophesied, until he came to Naioth in Ramah.

24 He also stripped off his clothes, and he also prophesied before Samuel, and lay down naked all that day and all that night. Therefore they say, “Is Saul also among the prophets?”

Summary

Saul now speaks openly to Jonathan and his servants of his desire to kill David. Jonathan, who delights in David, warns him to hide and then pleads his case before his father, reminding Saul that David has not sinned against him but risked his life to win great victories for Israel. Saul listens and swears that David will not be put to death, and for a time David returns to court. But after another victory over the Philistines, the tormenting spirit returns, and Saul again hurls his spear; David escapes into the night. Saul sends men to watch David's house and kill him in the morning, but Michal, David's wife, lowers him through a window and uses a household idol in the bed to deceive the messengers, buying him time to flee. David escapes to Samuel at Ramah, and when Saul sends messengers to seize him, the Spirit of God comes upon them and they prophesy. This happens to a second and third company, and finally to Saul himself, who strips off his clothes and lies prophesying all that day and night, so that people ask, “Is Saul also among the prophets?” Again and again human malice is overruled, and the Lord's anointed is preserved.

Main Characters

  • David — The Lord's anointed, now openly hunted, who escapes Saul's spear and messengers through the help of friends and the hand of God.
  • Saul — The king whose murderous intent toward David becomes open and relentless, yet is repeatedly thwarted, even when he himself is overcome by the Spirit.
  • Jonathan — Saul's son who defends David before his father and secures a temporary reprieve for his friend.
  • Michal — David's wife and Saul's daughter, who lowers David through a window and deceives the messengers to save his life.

Key Verse

1 Samuel 19:6 (WEB)

Saul listened to the voice of Jonathan: and Saul swore, “As Yahweh lives, he shall not be put to death.”

Lessons Learned

  • God can use ordinary people and ordinary courage to protect his purposes.
  • Hardened sin breaks even solemn vows and returns to its old hostility.
  • The Lord sovereignly overrules the schemes of those who oppose his anointed.
  • Faithful friends advocate for the innocent and stand against injustice.
  • No human power can finally thwart what God has determined to do.
  • Advocacy can stay the hand of harm. Jonathan pleads David's case and Saul swears, “As Yahweh lives, he shall not be put to death” (1 Samuel 19:6, WEB). Speaking up for the innocent can turn aside evil, at least for a time.
  • Unrepentant sin returns. Despite his oath, Saul again seeks “to pin David even to the wall with the spear” (1 Samuel 19:10, WEB). Apart from true repentance, old patterns of sin reassert themselves.
  • God protects through people. “Michal let David down through the window” (1 Samuel 19:12, WEB). The Lord often works his deliverance through the courage and quick thinking of his servants.
  • God can overrule any pursuer. “the Spirit of God came on him also, and he… prophesied” (1 Samuel 19:23, WEB). Even Saul is rendered powerless when God chooses to intervene.
  1. How does Jonathan intercede for David, and what makes his argument persuasive to Saul?
  2. Why does Saul break the oath he swore, and what does this reveal about the state of his heart?
  3. How do Michal's actions show both courage and resourcefulness in protecting David?
  4. What is the significance of the Spirit of God coming upon Saul's messengers and even Saul himself?
  5. When have you seen God protect or provide through unexpected people or means in your own life?
  1. Jonathan reminds Saul that David has not wronged him but has served him faithfully and risked his life for Israel, asking why Saul would sin against innocent blood (19:4-5). His appeal to justice and David's record briefly moves Saul to swear David will not die.
  2. Saul breaks his oath after the tormenting spirit returns and his old envy resurfaces (19:9-10). His repentance was shallow and circumstantial, not a true turning; without a changed heart, his solemn vow could not hold against his settled hostility.
  3. Michal lowers David through a window under cover of night and places a household idol in his bed to fool the messengers (19:12-16). Her quick, daring deception buys David the time he needs to escape, showing love expressed in decisive action.
  4. When the Spirit comes upon the messengers and then Saul, all of them prophesy and are kept from harming David (19:20-24). God demonstrates that he can disarm any pursuer; even the king is overruled, underscoring that David's safety rests in God's hands.
  5. This is a personal-application question. Invite members to recall times when help came through unlikely people or surprising circumstances, and to recognize God's hand in them. As leader, encourage gratitude and trust in God's faithful, often hidden, protection.

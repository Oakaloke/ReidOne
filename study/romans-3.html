Romans 3: Justified Freely by Grace

After proving that all are under sin and no one is righteous, Paul unveils the righteousness of God given through faith in Jesus Christ.

Romans 3 (WEB)

1 Then what advantage does the Jew have? Or what is the profit of circumcision?

2 Much in every way! Because first of all, they were entrusted with the oracles of God.

3 For what if some were without faith? Will their lack of faith nullify the faithfulness of God?

4 May it never be! Yes, let God be found true, but every man a liar. As it is written, “That you might be justified in your words, and might prevail when you come into judgment.”

5 But if our unrighteousness commends the righteousness of God, what will we say? Is God unrighteous who inflicts wrath? I speak like men do.

6 May it never be! For then how will God judge the world?

7 For if the truth of God through my lie abounded to his glory, why am I also still judged as a sinner?

8 Why not (as we are slanderously reported, and as some affirm that we say), “Let us do evil, that good may come?” Those who say so are justly condemned.

9 What then? Are we better than they? No, in no way. For we previously warned both Jews and Greeks, that they are all under sin.

10 As it is written, “There is no one righteous; no, not one.

11 There is no one who understands. There is no one who seeks after God.

12 They have all turned aside. They have together become unprofitable. There is no one who does good, no, not so much as one.”

13 “Their throat is an open tomb. With their tongues they have used deceit.” “The poison of vipers is under their lips”;

14 “whose mouth is full of cursing and bitterness.”

15 “Their feet are swift to shed blood.

16 Destruction and misery are in their ways.

17 The way of peace, they haven’t known.”

18 “There is no fear of God before their eyes.”

19 Now we know that whatever things the law says, it speaks to those who are under the law, that every mouth may be closed, and all the world may be brought under the judgment of God.

20 Because by the works of the law, no flesh will be justified in his sight. For through the law comes the knowledge of sin.

21 But now apart from the law, a righteousness of God has been revealed, being testified by the law and the prophets;

22 even the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ to all and on all those who believe. For there is no distinction,

23 for all have sinned, and fall short of the glory of God;

24 being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus;

25 whom God sent to be an atoning sacrifice, through faith in his blood, for a demonstration of his righteousness through the passing over of prior sins, in God’s forbearance;

26 to demonstrate his righteousness at this present time; that he might himself be just, and the justifier of him who has faith in Jesus.

27 Where then is the boasting? It is excluded. By what kind of law? Of works? No, but by a law of faith.

28 We maintain therefore that a man is justified by faith apart from the works of the law.

29 Or is God the God of Jews only? Isn’t he the God of Gentiles also? Yes, of Gentiles also,

30 since indeed there is one God who will justify the circumcised by faith, and the uncircumcised through faith.

31 Do we then nullify the law through faith? May it never be! No, we establish the law.

Summary

Paul anticipates objections: if all are under judgment, what advantage has the Jew? Much, for they were entrusted with the very words of God, and human faithlessness does not nullify God's faithfulness. He then drives home his case from Scripture, stringing together verses to prove that none is righteous, none understands, none seeks God; all have turned aside. The law's purpose is to stop every mouth and hold the whole world accountable to God, for by works of the law no one will be justified—through the law comes only the knowledge of sin. But now the turning point arrives: apart from the law, a righteousness of God has been revealed, attested by the law and the prophets, the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ for all who believe. There is no distinction, for all have sinned and fall short of God's glory, and all are justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus, whom God set forth as an atoning sacrifice. In this way God is shown to be both just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus. Boasting is excluded, for justification comes by faith apart from works, to Jew and Gentile alike, and faith does not nullify but upholds the law.

Key Figures

  • Paul — The apostle who answers objections, gathers Scripture to prove universal sin, and then announces the righteousness of God given freely through faith in Christ.
  • God the Justifier — The faithful God who set forth Christ as an atoning sacrifice, remaining just while justifying the one who trusts in Jesus.
  • Christ Jesus — The redemption and atoning sacrifice through faith in whose blood sinners are justified, demonstrating God's righteousness.
  • All humanity under sin — Jew and Greek alike, none righteous and none seeking God, every mouth stopped and the whole world accountable before God.

Lessons Learned

  • Scripture testifies that no one is righteous; all have turned aside from God.
  • The law cannot justify; it can only give knowledge of sin.
  • Justification is a free gift of grace, received through faith in Jesus Christ.
  • At the cross God is both perfectly just and the justifier of those who believe.
  • No one is righteous. "There is no one righteous; no, not one" (Romans 3:10, WEB). Scripture levels every claim of human goodness and prepares us for grace.
  • The law diagnoses, it cannot cure. "Through the law comes the knowledge of sin" (Romans 3:20, WEB). The law reveals our disease but offers no remedy; it points us beyond itself.
  • Justification is a free gift. We are "justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus" (Romans 3:24, WEB). Righteousness is given, not earned, at God's cost and not ours.
  • The cross keeps God just and merciful. God is shown "just, and the justifier of him who has faith in Jesus" (Romans 3:26, WEB). At the cross mercy and justice meet without compromise.
  1. How does Paul answer the objection that being under judgment robs the Jew of any advantage (3:1-4)?
  2. What is the cumulative point of the Scriptures Paul quotes in verses 10-18?
  3. Why can the law not justify anyone, and what can it actually do (3:20)?
  4. How does the cross allow God to be both "just" and "the justifier" (3:26)?
  5. How does it change you to know your standing with God is a free gift rather than something you achieve?
  1. Paul affirms that the Jews had the great advantage of being entrusted with God's words, and that human unfaithfulness cannot cancel God's faithfulness (3:1-4). God remains true even when people fail. This protects God's character and keeps the focus on his reliability, not our performance.
  2. The chain of quotations hammers home that sin is universal and total—no one righteous, no one who seeks God, corruption from throat to feet (3:10-18). Paul wants every reader to stop defending themselves. Only when we accept the diagnosis can we receive the cure.
  3. The law cannot justify because it sets a standard we have already broken; it can only reveal sin (3:20). It functions like a mirror that shows the dirt but cannot wash it away. This drives us to look outside ourselves to Christ.
  4. At the cross God did not ignore sin but dealt with it in Christ, the atoning sacrifice, so he can pardon the guilty without becoming unjust (3:24-26). Justice was satisfied and mercy extended in one act. Marvel together at a salvation that costs God everything and us nothing.
  5. This is a personal-application question. Invite members to reflect on how striving to earn God's approval has shaped them, and what rest looks like when righteousness is received as a gift. Encourage them to let this truth quiet anxiety and fuel gratitude.

Bible Study · History

1 Samuel

From a barren woman's prayer to the anointing of a shepherd-king, 1 Samuel charts Israel's move from judges to monarchy under God's sovereign hand.

← All 66 Books

Overview

First Samuel begins in personal grief and prayer. Hannah, childless and distressed, pours out her heart to the Lord and vows that if given a son she will dedicate him to God. The Lord answers, and Samuel is born and brought to serve at the tabernacle under Eli. While Eli's own sons are corrupt priests, the boy Samuel grows in favor with God and people, and the Lord calls him in the night, raising up a faithful prophet at a time when the word of the Lord was rare.

Israel suffers under the Philistines and even loses the ark of the covenant in battle, though God defends his own honor among the Philistine gods and the ark is returned. Samuel leads the people to repentance and serves as the last of the judges. Yet as he grows old, the people demand a king like the other nations. Though this rejects God's direct rule, the Lord grants their request and Samuel anoints Saul, a tall and impressive man from the tribe of Benjamin.

Saul's reign starts with promise but soon unravels through disobedience. He presumes to offer sacrifice rather than wait for Samuel, and later spares what God commanded to be destroyed, valuing the people's approval over the Lord's word. Samuel announces that the kingdom will be torn from him, declaring that to obey is better than sacrifice. God rejects Saul as king and sends Samuel to anoint a new and unlikely choice.

God directs Samuel to Bethlehem, where the youngest son of Jesse, David, is anointed, for the Lord looks not on outward appearance but on the heart. David enters Saul's service, famously defeats the giant Goliath in faith, and rises in popularity, which fuels Saul's jealousy. The rest of the book traces David's flight from a murderous Saul, his refusal to harm the Lord's anointed, and Saul's tragic end on Mount Gilboa, clearing the way for David's reign.

Key Verse

1 Samuel 16:7 (WEB)

But Yahweh said to Samuel, “Don’t look on his face, or on the height of his stature; because I have rejected him: for I see not as man sees; for man looks at the outward appearance, but Yahweh looks at the heart.”

As Samuel anoints David, God reveals the standard by which he chooses: not outward appearance but the heart.

The Big Movements

  • Samuel's rise (chs 1-7) — Hannah's prayer, Samuel's calling, and his leadership as prophet and judge.
  • Israel demands a king (chs 8-12) — The people ask for a king and Samuel anoints Saul.
  • Saul's failures (chs 13-15) — Saul's disobedience leads to his rejection as king.
  • David anointed and Goliath (chs 16-17) — David is chosen by God and defeats the Philistine giant.
  • David and Saul (chs 18-26) — Saul's jealousy and pursuit, and David's refusal to harm the Lord's anointed.
  • Saul's downfall (chs 27-31) — Saul consults a medium and falls in battle on Gilboa.

Key Figures

  • Samuel — The prophet, priest, and last judge who anoints both Saul and David.
  • Hannah — The faithful mother whose prayer and dedication bring Samuel into God's service.
  • Saul — Israel's first king, gifted but disobedient, rejected for valuing approval over obedience.
  • David — The shepherd anointed king, a man after God's own heart who trusts the Lord against Goliath.
  • Jonathan — Saul's son whose loyal friendship with David models selfless love.

Pointing to Christ

David, the shepherd anointed and yet waiting for the throne, foreshadows Jesus, the Good Shepherd and anointed King who is rejected before he reigns. David's victory over Goliath pictures Christ defeating the enemy on behalf of a people who could not save themselves, and God's promise to David's line points to the eternal King.

Big Lessons

  • God hears and answers heartfelt prayer, as Hannah discovered.
  • Obedience matters more to God than outward religious acts.
  • The Lord judges by the heart, not by appearance or stature.
  • Faith, not size or strength, wins God's battles.
  • Seeking the approval of people above God leads to ruin.
  • We can trust God's timing rather than seizing what is not yet ours.
  1. What does Hannah's prayer teach you about bringing your deepest needs to God?
  2. Why was Israel's demand for a king a rejection of God's rule, and how do we do the same?
  3. How do Saul's failures warn against valuing people's approval over obedience?
  4. What does it mean that God looks on the heart rather than appearance?
  5. How does David's confrontation with Goliath shape your view of facing overwhelming odds?
  6. Why did David refuse to harm Saul, and what does that teach about trusting God's timing?

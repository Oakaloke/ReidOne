Bible Study · Major Prophets

Daniel

Stories of faithful courage in a foreign empire and visions of God's everlasting kingdom that outlasts every throne of earth.

← All 66 Books

Overview

Daniel and his friends were young men of Judah taken to Babylon and trained to serve a pagan king. From the start they resolved not to defile themselves, and God gave them wisdom and favor. The book's first half tells gripping stories of faith under pressure, as God preserves his servants and humbles the proudest rulers of the earth.

Daniel interprets dreams that no one else can, revealing that God alone unveils mysteries and raises up and removes kings. His three friends survive a blazing furnace, and Daniel himself is delivered from a den of lions, demonstrating that the living God rescues those who trust him and that his kingdom will never be destroyed.

The book's second half turns to Daniel's own visions of beasts, kingdoms, and the end of the age. These visions can be mysterious, but their message is clear and reassuring: human empires rise and fall, yet the Ancient of Days reigns, and one like a son of man receives an everlasting dominion that all peoples will serve.

Daniel models a life of prayer, integrity, and trust in a hostile culture. Even when he does not understand all that he sees, he holds fast to God's character and sovereignty. The book assures God's people in every age that history is moving toward the triumph of God's unshakable kingdom.

Key Verse

Daniel 7:13-14 (WEB)

I saw in the night visions, and behold, there came with the clouds of the sky one like a son of man, and he came even to the ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. There was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all the peoples, nations, and languages should serve him: his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed.

Daniel sees one like a son of man given everlasting dominion, a title Jesus claimed for himself as the coming King.

The Big Movements

  • Faithful in Babylon (chs. 1-2) — Daniel and his friends stay true, and God reveals the king's dream of a stone that fills the earth.
  • Fire and humbling (chs. 3-4) — The furnace deliverance and Nebuchadnezzar's humbling show God rules over kings.
  • Writing on the wall (chs. 5-6) — Belshazzar is judged, and Daniel is delivered from the lions' den.
  • Beasts and the Son of Man (chs. 7-8) — Visions of empires give way to the Ancient of Days and an everlasting kingdom.
  • Prayer and the seventy weeks (ch. 9) — Daniel's confession is met with a prophecy of God's redemptive timetable.
  • Final visions (chs. 10-12) — A vision of conflict and the end, with the promise of resurrection and reward.

Key Figures

  • Daniel — The faithful exile and prophet, a man of prayer and integrity who interprets God's revelation.
  • Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego — Daniel's friends who refuse to bow and are saved from the fiery furnace.
  • Nebuchadnezzar — The Babylonian king humbled until he acknowledges the Most High rules over all.
  • The Ancient of Days — God enthroned in glory, who gives dominion to one like a son of man.
  • The angel Gabriel — The messenger sent to help Daniel understand his visions.

Pointing to Christ

Daniel's vision of one like a son of man coming with the clouds of heaven, receiving an everlasting kingdom, became Jesus' favorite title for himself. The stone cut without human hands that shatters the kingdoms of earth points to Christ's unshakable reign. In the fiery furnace a fourth figure walks with the faithful, a glimpse of God's saving presence, and the promise of resurrection in chapter twelve is secured by the risen Lord.

Big Lessons

  • Faithfulness to God matters most in small, daily decisions.
  • God is sovereign over every ruler and empire, however powerful.
  • Courage flows from trusting God's character, not knowing the outcome.
  • Prayer and humility mark a life that honors God in exile.
  • God's kingdom is the one kingdom that will never be destroyed.
  • We can hold fast even when we do not understand all God is doing.
  1. What 'small' decisions test your faithfulness the way Daniel's diet tested his?
  2. How does God's sovereignty over kings encourage you about today's world?
  3. Where do you need courage like Daniel's friends, who trusted God 'even if' he did not rescue them?
  4. What does Daniel's life of prayer teach you about living faithfully in a secular culture?
  5. How does the vision of the son of man shape your hope for the future?
  6. How can you hold fast to God when his purposes feel mysterious to you?

Bible Study · History

2 Kings

The story of two kingdoms drifting toward exile, marked by the miracles of Elisha, a few faithful reformers, and the long-suffering patience of God.

← All 66 Books

Overview

Second Kings continues the story where the first left off, beginning with the dramatic departure of Elijah, who is taken up to heaven in a whirlwind, and the passing of his ministry to Elisha. Elisha carries out a remarkable series of miracles that reveal God's power and compassion: purifying water, multiplying oil for a widow, raising a boy to life, feeding a hundred men, and healing the foreign commander Naaman of leprosy. These signs show that God is at work among his people even as the nation declines.

Alongside Elisha's ministry, the book traces the parade of kings in both Israel and Judah, evaluating each by faithfulness to the Lord. Jehu is raised up to purge the house of Ahab and Baal worship from Israel, yet he does not fully follow God. In Judah, the wicked queen Athaliah seizes power before the boy Joash is rescued and crowned. Through it all, the northern kingdom in particular hardens in idolatry despite repeated prophetic warnings.

The northern kingdom of Israel finally falls. Because of persistent idolatry and refusal to heed the prophets, God allows Assyria to conquer Samaria and carry the ten northern tribes into exile, a sobering fulfillment of the warnings given for generations. The narrator explains plainly that this disaster came because Israel had sinned against the Lord and worshiped other gods, forsaking the covenant that had set them apart.

Judah survives longer but follows the same path. There are bright moments, especially the reforms of Hezekiah, who trusts God against Assyria, and Josiah, who rediscovers the Book of the Law and leads a sweeping renewal. Yet the nation's heart does not lasting change, and after a succession of evil kings, Babylon besieges Jerusalem, destroys the temple, and carries Judah into exile. The book ends in the shadow of judgment, with a small note of hope as an exiled king is shown kindness.

Context at a Glance

Author
Anonymous; tradition associates Jeremiah
Written
Events around 850-560 BC; compiled later
Genre
Historical narrative
Audience
Israel and Judah, especially the exiles seeking to understand their fall
Central theme
Persistent unfaithfulness brings judgment, yet God remains faithful to his promise

Key Verse

2 Kings 17:7 (WEB)

It was so, because the children of Israel had sinned against Yahweh their God, who brought them up out of the land of Egypt from under the hand of Pharaoh king of Egypt, and had feared other gods,

This verse states the book's central explanation: the exile came because the people sinned against the Lord and turned to other gods.

The Big Movements

  • Elijah to Elisha (chs 1-2) — Elijah is taken to heaven and Elisha receives his prophetic mantle.
  • Elisha's ministry (chs 3-8) — Miracles of provision, healing, and life reveal God's power and mercy.
  • Jehu and the kings (chs 9-16) — Jehu purges Baal worship, but both kingdoms continue in sin.
  • Fall of Israel (ch 17) — The northern kingdom is exiled to Assyria for its idolatry.
  • Hezekiah and Josiah (chs 18-23) — Faithful reformers in Judah trust God and renew the covenant.
  • Fall of Judah (chs 24-25) — Babylon destroys Jerusalem and carries Judah into exile.

Key Figures

  • Elisha — The prophet who succeeds Elijah and performs many miracles of mercy and power.
  • Naaman — The Aramean commander healed of leprosy after humbling himself to obey God's word.
  • Jehu — The king anointed to destroy Ahab's house and Baal worship in Israel.
  • Hezekiah — A faithful king of Judah who trusts God against Assyria and pursues reform.
  • Josiah — The reforming king who rediscovers the Law and renews the covenant.

Pointing to Christ

The faithful prophets and reforming kings point beyond themselves to the perfect Prophet and King, Jesus. Naaman's cleansing pictures grace reaching the nations, and the failure of even Judah's best kings to secure lasting renewal deepens the longing for the Messiah, who would establish a kingdom that exile and death cannot destroy.

Big Lessons

  • God works powerfully and mercifully even in days of decline.
  • Humble obedience, like Naaman's, brings the healing pride cannot.
  • Persistent sin and ignoring God's warnings lead to judgment.
  • Faithful reform begins with rediscovering and obeying God's word.
  • Outward reforms cannot change a heart that remains unrepentant.
  • Even in judgment, God preserves a thread of hope for his promise.
  1. What do Elisha's miracles reveal about God's character toward the needy?
  2. Why was Naaman's healing tied to humble obedience, and where does pride hinder you?
  3. What warnings does the fall of Israel sound for God's people in every age?
  4. How do Hezekiah's and Josiah's reforms inspire renewal in your own life?
  5. Why were even great reforms unable to prevent Judah's exile?
  6. How does the longing left at the end of 2 Kings point you to Christ?

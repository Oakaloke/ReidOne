Bible Study · History

Joshua

After forty years of wandering, a new generation enters the land God promised, led by Joshua and the faithfulness of the Lord.

← All 66 Books

Overview

Joshua opens at a turning point. Moses has died, and the Lord commissions Joshua to lead Israel across the Jordan into the land promised to Abraham. The repeated charge is to be strong and courageous, meditating on the law day and night. Faith is tested at once: spies are sent to Jericho, Rahab hides them and confesses the Lord, and the people prepare to cross the river that bars their way into the inheritance.

God parts the Jordan as he once parted the Red Sea, and the nation passes through on dry ground, setting up memorial stones. The conquest begins not with military strategy but with worship and obedience: Jericho's walls fall after Israel marches and shouts at God's command. Yet sin in the camp through Achan brings defeat at Ai, showing that victory depends on holiness, not numbers, until the matter is judged and dealt with.

Campaign by campaign, Israel takes the central, southern, and northern regions, though the gradual nature of the conquest is honest about land still to be claimed. The Gibeonites secure peace by deception, and even the sun stands still as God fights for his people. The narrative stresses again and again that it is the Lord who gives the victories, fulfilling what he swore to the ancestors.

The book's second half divides the land among the tribes by lot, assigning cities of refuge and towns for the Levites. The eastern tribes return home, a misunderstanding over an altar is resolved peacefully, and Joshua gathers Israel for farewell addresses. At Shechem he renews the covenant and calls the people to choose whom they will serve, leaving them with his famous resolve to serve the Lord.

Key Verse

Joshua 1:9 (WEB)

Haven’t I commanded you? Be strong and courageous. Don’t be afraid, neither be dismayed: for Yahweh your God is with you wherever you go.”

God's charge to Joshua grounds courage not in his own strength but in the Lord's promised presence wherever he goes.

The Big Movements

  • Entering the land (chs 1-5) — Joshua is commissioned, Rahab helps the spies, and Israel crosses the Jordan into the land.
  • Conquest (chs 6-12) — Jericho and Ai, then central, southern, and northern campaigns as God gives victory.
  • Dividing the land (chs 13-21) — The territory is allotted to the tribes, with cities of refuge and Levitical towns.
  • Eastern tribes and the altar (ch 22) — The eastern tribes return home and a near conflict over an altar is resolved.
  • Covenant renewal (chs 23-24) — Joshua's farewell charges and the renewal of covenant at Shechem.

Key Figures

  • Joshua — Moses' successor who leads Israel into the land with courage and obedience to God's word.
  • Rahab — The Canaanite woman of Jericho who shelters the spies and confesses faith in the Lord.
  • Achan — The man whose hidden sin brings defeat at Ai, showing the cost of disobedience.
  • Caleb — The faithful spy of the older generation who claims his promised inheritance at Hebron.
  • The Lord — The true conqueror who fights for Israel and keeps his covenant promises.

Pointing to Christ

Joshua, who shares the name later given as Jesus, leads God's people into their promised rest, foreshadowing the greater Joshua who leads us into eternal rest. Rahab's faith places a Gentile in the line of the Messiah, and the cities of refuge picture the safety found in Christ from the judgment we deserve.

Big Lessons

  • Courage flows from trusting God's presence and his word, not from our own resources.
  • God keeps every promise he makes, even when fulfillment takes generations.
  • Hidden sin affects the whole community and must be brought into the light.
  • Faith, like Rahab's, can come from unexpected people and transform a life.
  • Worship and obedience precede victory in God's economy.
  • Each new generation must choose to serve the Lord for themselves.
  1. Where in your life do you most need to hear the call to be strong and courageous?
  2. What does the crossing of the Jordan teach about stepping forward in faith before the way is clear?
  3. How does Rahab's story expand your sense of who God welcomes into his people?
  4. Why did Achan's sin have such serious consequences, and what does it say about community?
  5. What spiritual rest does Joshua's leadership point us toward in Christ?
  6. What would it look like for you to say with Joshua, 'As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord'?
